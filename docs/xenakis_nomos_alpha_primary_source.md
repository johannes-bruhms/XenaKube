"Organization Outside-Time
Consider a set U and a comparison of U by U (a product U × U) denoted ψ(U, f). Then ψ(U, f) ⊂ U × U and for all pairs (u, u_f) ∈ U × U such that u, u_f ∈ U, either (u, u_f) ∈ ψ(U, f), or (u, u_f) ∉ ψ(U, f). It is reflexive and (u ~ u_f) ⇒ (u_f ~ u); (u ~ u_f and u_f ~ u'') ⇒ u ~ u'' for u, u'', u_f ∈ ψ(U, f).

Thus ψ(U, f) is an equivalence class. In particular if U is isomorphic to the set Q of rational numbers, then u ~ u_f if |u - u_f| ≤ Δu_f for arbitrary Δu_f.

Now we define ψ(U, f) as the set of weak values of U, ψ(U, m) as the set of average values, and ψ(U, p) as the strong values. We then have ψ = ψ(U, f) ∪ ψ(U, m) ∪ ψ(U, p) ⊂ U × U where ψ is the quotient set of U by ψ.

The subsets of ψ may intersect or be disjoint, and may or may not form a partition of U × U. Here ψ(U, f) → ψ(U, m) → ψ(U, p) are ordered by the relation → in such a way that the elements of ψ(U, f) are smaller than those of ψ(U, m) and those of ψ(U, m) are smaller than those of ψ(U, p). Then ψ(U, f) ∩ ψ(U, m) = ∅, ψ(U, m) ∩ ψ(U, p) = ∅.

In each of these subsets we define four new equivalence relations and therefore four sub-classes: ψ^i(U, f) with u^i ~ (u^i)' if and only if |u^i - (u^i)'| ≤ Δu^i with u^i, (u^i)' ∈ ψ(U, f) for i = 1,2,3,4, with ψ^1(U, f) ⊂ ψ(U, f) and ψ^4(U, f) → ψ^2(U, f) → ψ^3(U, f) → ψ^4(U, f) ordered by the same relation →. The same equivalence relations and sub-classes are defined for ψ(U, m) and ψ(U, p).

For simplification we write u^i = {u: u ∈ ψ^i(U, f)}, and the same for u^m and u^p.

In the same way, equivalence sub-classes are created in two other sets, G and D. Here U represents the set of time values, G the set of intensity values, and D the set of density values with U = {u1,u2,u3,u4}, G = {g1,g2,g3,g4}, D = {d1,d2,d3,d4} for i,j,k = 1,2,3,4.

Take part of the triple product U × G × D composed of the points (u_i, g_i, d_i). Consider the paths V1: {u1,g1,d1}, V2: {u1,g2,d1}, … VS: {(u1,u2,u3,u4),(g1,g2,g3,g4),(d1,d2,d3,d4)} for i=1,2,3,4. VS will be a subset of the triple product U × G × D split into 4³ = 64 different points.

In each of these subsets choose a new subset K_j defined by the n points K_j (j=1,2,…,n and λ=V1,V2,…,VS). These n points are considered as the n vertices of a regular polyhedron. Consider the transformations which leave the polyhedron unchanged, that is, its corresponding group.

To sum up, we have the following chain of inclusions: ω ∈ S^n_s ⊂ K_j ⊂ λ ⊂ ψ ⊆ U × G × D. (element vertex of set of path λ of the poly- vertices (subset of U × G × D) hedron of the polyhedron)

Consider the two other sets H (pitch) and X (sonic material, way of playing, etc.). Form the product H × X × C in which C is the set of n forms or complexes or sound types C_i (i=1,2,…,n); for example, a cloud of sound-points or a cloud of glissandi. Map the product H × X × C onto the vertices of the polyhedron K_j.

1. The complexes C_i traverse the fixed vertices and thus produce group transformations; we call this operation θ_0.
2. The complexes C_i are attached to corresponding vertices which remain fixed, but the H × X traverse the vertices, also producing group transformations; this operation is called θ_1.
3. The product H × X × C traverses the vertices thus producing the group transformations of the polyhedron; we call this operation θ_n because the product can change definition at each transformation of the polyhedron.

Organization In-Time
The last mapping will be inscribed in time in two possible ways in order to manifest the peculiarities of this polyhedral group or the symmetric group to which it is isomorphic: operation t_0—the vertices of the polyhedron are expressed successively (model of the symmetric group); operation t_1—the vertices are expressed simultaneously (n simultaneous voices).

Product t_0 × θ_0: The vertices K_j are expressed successively with: 1. only one sonic complex C_i, always the same one, for example, a cloud of sound-points only, 2. several sonic complexes, at most n, in one-to-one attachment with indices of vertices K_i, 3. several sonic complexes whose successive appearances express the operations of the polyhedral group, the vertices i (defined by U × G × D) always appearing in the same order, 4. several sonic complexes always in the same order while the order of the vertices i reproduces the group transformations, 5. several sonic complexes transforming independently from the vertices of the polyhedron.

Product t_0 × θ_1: The list which this product generates may be obtained from the preceding one by substituting H × X in place of C_i. Product t_0 × θ_n: This list may be readily established. Case t_1 and θ_1 is obtained from the preceding ones by analogy.

To these in-time operational products one ought to be able to add in-space operations when, for example, the sonic sources are distributed in space in significant manner, as in Terréktorh or Nomos gamma."
